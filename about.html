About us

A power company built around a waste problem.

Who we are

We started with the smoke, not the megawatts.

Every year the rice and corn harvest in Lampang and Lamphun leaves roughly 1.9 million tonnes of husk, cob and straw in the fields. Most of it used to be burned where it lay, which is why the province spends February and March under haze. The company was formed in 2012 on a straightforward proposition: pay farmers for that residue, burn it under permit in a boiler with proper emissions control, and sell the electricity.

Fourteen years on, ModPao runs 128 MW of installed capacity across two independently dispatchable units, employs 214 people — most from within 20 km of the gate — and buys fuel from 1,400 registered farms and 34 mills inside a 60 km radius. The plant has been on the grid since March 2016 and closed 2025 at 96.1% availability, which is the number our offtakers care about most.

We are not a developer. We own and operate one asset, and the business is deliberately narrow: run the units reliably, keep the fuel chain local and honestly priced, stay well inside the emissions permit, and be a plant the four surrounding sub-districts are content to live next to. Everything on this site — availability history, emissions averages, outage plans, the fuel price — is published because those are the things an offtaker and a neighbour both need to be able to check.

How it was built

Fifteen years from a provincial study to a recontracting decision.

2011
Feasibility
A provincial study finds 1.9 million tonnes of un-used crop residue burned in the open in Lampang and Lamphun each year. Site screening begins in Mae Tha.
2012
Incorporation & land
ModPao Power incorporated in February. 46 hectares assembled from 31 separate title deeds, all by negotiated purchase. EIA scoping opens with three public hearings.
2013
Permits & offtake
EIA approved with 42 monitoring conditions. A 25-year power purchase agreement is signed for the first capacity tranche.
2014
Financial close
THB 9.4 billion project financing closed with a four-bank syndicate at 70:30 debt to equity. EPC contract awarded on a turnkey basis.
2015
Construction
Peak workforce of 2,100. The fuel-supply team registers the first 600 farms and sets the opening posted price for rice husk.
2016
Commercial operation
Unit 1 synchronised in March, Unit 2 in November — 128 MW gross. First full-year availability 91.4%.
2018
Certification
ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 awarded. The ash-return programme is formalised, with free lab analysis per batch for registered suppliers.
2019
Fuel yard expansion
Covered storage raised to 42,000 tonnes — nine weeks of burn — sized against a 1-in-20-year poor harvest. Thailand Energy Award received.
2021
Bag filter retrofit
THB 310 million retrofit on both stacks cuts the particulate annual average from 41 to 18 mg/m³, against a 120 mg/m³ permit limit.
2023
Safety milestone
ISO 45001 certification and three consecutive years without a lost-time injury across plant and fuel logistics.
2024
Certificates
Registered for I-REC issuance against metered generation from both units, with serial numbers reported to buyers quarterly.
2025
Record year
948 GWh net at 96.1% availability, the best in the plant's history. THB 34 million paid to local fuel suppliers.
2026
Recontracting
28 MW is released to the market as the 2016 offtake tranche approaches term in June 2027.

How we staff

Vacancies are posted at the gate office for two weeks before going anywhere else, so people living inside the four sub-districts see them first. Apprenticeships run with two vocational colleges in Lampang, and 23 of the current shift operators started that way.

Governance

Committees, policies and the channels that exist to be used against management rather than by it.

Board of directors

Seven directors, three of them independent. Meets quarterly, with an annual strategy session. No executive holds the chair.

Audit committee

Three independent directors. Appoints the external auditor, reviews the quarterly accounts and owns the internal-audit plan.

Risk & HSE committee

Reviews the plant risk register, all incidents regardless of severity, and outage plans before they are filed.

Fuel pricing committee

Sets the posted price weekly. Includes a standing representative of the supplying cooperatives, with a published methodology.

Anti-bribery policy

Zero facilitation payments, gift register for anything above THB 3,000, annual training for all staff who deal with suppliers or officials.

Whistleblower channel

Reports go to the audit committee chair directly, bypassing management. Anonymous submission accepted, retaliation is a dismissal offence.

Supplier code of conduct

No child labour, verified land tenure for fuel sourcing, no open burning on supplying plots. Applies to all 1,400 registered suppliers.

Community grievance procedure

A 24-hour duty line, every call logged with a written answer within seven days, and the register summarised in the annual report.

In the community

A plant you can walk into.

Four sub-districts sit inside two kilometres of the stack. The programmes below are not sponsorship — they are the terms on which the plant operates, and the community fund is allocated by sub-district heads rather than by us.

Scholarships

42 per year

Awarded across four surrounding sub-districts, from lower-secondary to vocational level, with no requirement to work for the company afterwards.

Community fund

1% of net profit

Allocated by a committee of sub-district heads, not by the company. Has funded a health-centre extension, two water systems and road resurfacing.

Open gate days

Twice monthly

First and third Saturday. School groups, farmers and residents walk the plant with an engineer. Air-quality readings are posted at the gate.

Ash return

18,000 t / year

Bottom and fly ash offered back to registered fuel suppliers free of charge as a potassium and silica soil amendment, with analysis per batch.

Dry-season water

Since 2018

Plant tankers supply drinking water to four villages during the March–May shortage, on request from the sub-district administration.

Emergency response

On call

The plant fire crew and ambulance respond to incidents within the sub-district under a standing agreement with the local administration.
