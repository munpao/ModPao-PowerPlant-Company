Fuel supply

A fuel supply nobody else is bidding for.

Why residue

Residue, not crops.

Rice husk, corn cob and bagasse are all residues of a food crop that has already been grown, harvested and sold for something else. The plant is not competing with a food buyer, and the price it pays is not chasing a commodity market it can't afford to lose to.

It also removes both the commodity-price exposure of imported fuel and the open-field burning that used to follow each harvest in the province. That was the point of building the plant in the first place.

Fuel mix, 2025

Blend targets are set weekly by the fuel supply team against a calorific-value spec and an ash spec. Four feeds, three feed lines to the boiler hoppers.

Where it comes from

  • Corn cobHighland farms, collected after the November harvest

  • Wood chipCertified plantation residue, used as a shoulder fuel

Posted price, 2026/27 season

Fixed until 31 March 2027, published weekly to all registered suppliers. Anything above the posted price is a bilateral agreement — the fuel pricing committee reviews any exceptions monthly.

Rice huskTHB 1,150per tonne, delivered

All prices assume moisture below 12% and ash below 22%, measured at the gate weighbridge. A moisture premium is paid for consistently dry deliveries; a fuel that arrives at >18% moisture is either dried on site at the supplier's cost or rejected.

Supplier programme

1,400 registered farms, inside 60 km.

The registered supplier list is the fuel-security perimeter. Every registered supplier signs the supplier code of conduct (no child labour, verified land tenure, no open burning on supplying plots) and gets weighbridge access, on-farm collection scheduling and an ash-return account.
