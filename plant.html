The plant

Two circulating fluidised-bed units on 46 hectares.

Technical specification

Two independently dispatchable units, sized to the fuel catchment. The design margin is narrow on capacity and wide on emissions — the reverse of an early-generation biomass plant.

  • Installed capacity128 MW gross · 118 MW net
  • Units2 × 64 MW, independently dispatchable
  • BoilerCirculating fluidised bed, 2 × 280 t/h
  • Steam conditions540 °C / 98 bar
  • TurbineCondensing, single reheat
  • CoolingMechanical draught, closed circuit
  • Grid connection115 kV double circuit to Mae Tha substation
  • Emissions controlBag filter + SNCR, CEMS on both stacks
  • Fuel handlingCovered storage 42,000 t · 3 feed lines
  • Site area46 ha, including 11 ha fuel yard
  • Commercial operationUnit 1 — Mar 2016 · Unit 2 — Nov 2016
  • Offtake25-year PPA, first tranche to Jun 2027
Why CFB, not stoker

Circulating fluidised bed handles the mixed rice-husk / corn-cob / bagasse blend the local supply chain actually produces, tolerates fuel moisture up to 45%, and keeps NOx down without post-combustion catalyst. The plant was specified around the fuel it would burn, not around a Western wood-pellet baseline.

From residue to grid

Four steps from the field to the substation. Everything on the plant is instrumented, and everything the CEMS sees is on the Environment page.

01

Collection

Residue is bought at a posted price from farms and mills within 60 km, weighed and moisture-tested at the gate.

02

Storage & feed

42,000 tonnes under cover, enough for nine weeks. Three feed lines blend to a target calorific value.

03

Combustion & steam

Two CFB boilers raise steam at 540 °C driving condensing turbines, with bag filters and SNCR on the flue path.

04

Dispatch & return

Power exports on the 115 kV double circuit. Ash returns to suppliers as a soil amendment.

Control room

Two units, one DCS, three shifts.

The plant runs on a modern distributed control system: one DCS instance supervising both boiler/turbine units plus fuel handling, the CEMS instrument chain and the 115 kV switchyard. Operators sit through a three-shift roster with a shift engineer, a boiler operator and a turbine operator per unit on every shift.

Field-side, the boiler and turbine sit behind a segregated OT network. The historian mirrors the DCS tags out to a separate corporate segment for the environmental returns and the availability reports the offtaker sees.

Full generation history is published.

Availability by month, planned outages and forced-outage rate for every month the plant has been on the grid — that's the operational record any offtaker needs to run a due-diligence on.
