The plant

Two units, 46 hectares, on the grid since March 2016.

Two circulating fluidised-bed boilers raise steam at 540°C to drive independently-dispatchable turbines. Bag filters and continuous stack monitoring keep emissions well inside the province's permit — the numbers are published, not just promised.

Neighbours

An open gate, twice a month.

Four sub-districts sit inside two kilometres of the stack. On the first and third Saturday, school groups, farmers and residents walk the plant with an engineer — the air-quality readings for that day are posted at the gate before the tour starts.

Fuel is bought at a posted price from 1,400 registered farms and 34 mills inside a 60 km radius: rice husk, corn cob and bagasse — residue that used to be burned where it lay.

Environment

Ash back to the soil.

Bottom and fly ash — around 18,000 tonnes a year — goes back to registered fuel suppliers free of charge as a potassium and silica soil amendment, with a lab analysis per batch. It closes the loop the plant was built to close: the harvest residue leaves the farm as fuel and comes back as amendment, instead of leaving the air as smoke.

Particulate matter at the stack averages 18 mg/m³ against a 120 mg/m³ permit limit — a number that's checked continuously, not sampled once a year.
